## About — What We Believe

**Eyebrow:** Our faith
**H1:** Apostolic. Ancient. Alive today.

The Holy Apostolic Catholic Assyrian Church of the East is one of the oldest continuous Christian traditions in the world, tracing its founding to the apostolic mission of St Thomas, St Bartholomew, and St Addai in the first century.

**What sets us apart:**
- **Apostolic succession** — an unbroken line of bishops reaching back to the early church.
- **The Holy Raza** — our Divine Liturgy, rooted in ancient Syriac Christian worship, celebrated today in English for our congregation.
- **The cross without the corpus** — you'll notice our cross stands plain, on three steps representing Golgotha, rather than depicting the crucified Christ — a distinct visual and theological tradition within Eastern Christianity.

**The Nicene Creed**
The Nicene Creed is a mirror of our faith — it is what we believe, plainly and completely. Rather than summarising it in our own words, this page should display the Creed itself in full, so visitors can read exactly what the parish holds to be true.

*(Build note for Cursor: this should be the centrepiece of the page, not a footnote — set the Creed text in a distinct, readable block, ideally with the Fraunces display type for weight. Use the full traditional text as recited in the Holy Raza; confirm the exact translation/wording the parish uses, since there are minor textual variations between traditions.)*

We are not the same as the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, or other Eastern-rite churches — though we share deep historical roots with all of Christendom. If you're curious about exactly how, we're glad to talk about it after a service.

**An important note on the Holy Raza**
The Holy Raza is the Divine Liturgy of the church, and Holy Communion within it is reserved for those who have been baptised in an apostolic church. This isn't a barrier we put up for newcomers — it's the nature of the sacrament itself.

If you're exploring the faith or simply curious, you are genuinely welcome to attend and observe the Holy Raza. But the place we'd most encourage a first-time visitor to start is **Bible Study or Friday Night Preaching** — these are built for exactly that: asking questions, learning, and getting to know the community, with no expectations attached.

*(Build note for Cursor: this means the site's "plan a visit" framing should point newcomers toward Bible Study / Friday Night Preaching as the primary invitation, not the Holy Raza. The Holy Raza should be presented with reverence as the heart of parish worship, not as an outreach event.)*

**Accessible Liturgy**
On the second Sunday of every month at 5:00pm, we hold an Accessible Liturgy specifically designed for children and adults with autism or other special needs, along with their families. With dimmed lighting, lowered sound, and a calmer, sensory-friendly atmosphere, it's built so that everyone can take part in prayer and worship comfortably, surrounded by a welcoming community.

**What to expect**
Our services are conducted entirely in English. You don't need any background in the Assyrian Church of the East to follow along — there will be people nearby happy to help you find your place. Dress is smart-casual; there's no strict dress code. Children are welcome throughout the service, and a children's area / Sunday school runs [during the service / separately — confirm].

## Guest Guide — Attending a Baptism or Wedding

**Eyebrow:** For guests
**H1:** Joining us for a baptism or wedding

If you've been invited to a baptism or wedding at Sts Peter & Paul and aren't a regular member of the parish, this page is for you — what to expect, and what we ask in the house of God.

**What to expect**
The service follows the traditional liturgical rites of the Assyrian Church of the East, conducted in English. It will likely run longer than a typical Western church service — this reflects the richness of the rite, not a delay. Clergy and family will guide the key moments; you don't need to know what's coming next.

**What we ask of guests**
- **Dress respectfully.** This is a place of worship — modest, smart-casual attire is appropriate. [Confirm any specific guidance the parish wants stated explicitly, e.g. covered shoulders, no overly casual wear.]
- **Phones on silent.** Please avoid calls or loud notifications during the service.
- **Photography and video.** [Confirm the parish's actual policy — many churches restrict guest photography during key liturgical moments and prefer it be left to an appointed photographer. State clearly once confirmed.]
- **Movement during the service.** Please remain seated unless invited to move forward (e.g. for Communion, which is reserved for those baptised in an apostolic church — see note below). If you need to step out, do so quietly via the rear entrance.
- **Communion.** As with the regular Holy Raza, Communion is reserved for those baptised in an apostolic church. Guests who are not eligible to receive are welcome to remain seated respectfully during this part of the service.

**After the service**
Guests are warmly invited to stay for any fellowship or celebration that follows — this is part of the joy of the occasion, and a lovely way to share in the family's day.
